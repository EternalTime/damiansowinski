pyCoop

What does it take for a crowd to agree? Strip the question to its skeleton: \(N\) players on a graph, each carrying a continuous action in \([-1, 1]\) and a binary opinion — a thing they are doing and a thing they believe, with no guarantee the two line up. Actions drift toward opinions; every so often a player compares their action against their neighborhood’s, and if they are committed to a side yet poorly coordinated with the crowd, they change their mind. The group wins when every action lands on the same side. From these spare rules comes a zoo of outcomes — clean consensus, fraught standoffs where every player is locally content while the group as a whole has failed, obdurate jams pinned on a lone holdout — all governed by a single timescale and a pair of thresholds.

pyCoop implements this model on arbitrary graphs. The dynamics are the exact embedded chain of the continuous-time model — actions drift toward opinions, opinions flip when a player is committed but poorly coordinated — with the inter-event clock exposed as a switch, so the exponential law and a fixed-step variant can be compared head to head. The observables quantify what a state is — its agreement, its fraughtness, its obdurateness — and the visualization tools turn trajectories into pictures and film.

If you’re new here, start with Getting Started, then work through the guides in order — the dynamics first, then the observables that classify what the dynamics produce, then the tools for looking at it all.
