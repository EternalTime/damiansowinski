The Dynamics

A cooperation game is a piecewise-deterministic random process — ballistic drift punctuated by randomly clocked decisions. Nothing decision-relevant happens between events: positions are a deterministic function of the elapsed time and the headings, and only the flip rule reads the state, and only at decision instants. So the model collapses losslessly to an embedded chain in which one uniformly random player acts at each exponentially spaced event, and pyCoop implements that chain directly — no event queue, no lost information. The model is the Agreement-Position Emigration model of Conrad and Tabor (2024); the notation here follows the socio-physics recasting of it.

The update sequence

Let there be \(N\) agents on a connected graph with adjacency matrix \(A_{ij}\), without self-loops. Each agent carries two degrees of freedom — a continuous action \(\sigma_i \in [-1, 1]\) and a binary opinion \(q_i \in \{-1, +1\}\). The picture to hold in your head is a person who both believes something and is currently doing something about it, and the two need not agree.

On each step draw two random variables,

\[\tau \sim \mathrm{Exp}\!\left[\tfrac{N}{T}\right], \qquad j \sim \mathcal{U}_N,\]

the first setting how long we wait, the second picking who gets to act. Every action drifts toward its opinion and is clipped at the walls,

\[\sigma_i \mapsto \min(\max(\sigma_i + q_i\,\tau,\, -1),\, 1),\]

and then player \(j\) alone compares their action to their neighborhood’s. The local mean action

\[\bar\sigma_j = \frac{1}{d_j + 1}\Big(\sigma_j + \sum_k A_{jk}\,\sigma_k\Big)\]

counts the player alongside their neighbors — an agent listens to the crowd but also weighs themselves, which is why the prefactor is \(1/(d_j+1)\) rather than \(1/d_j\). The opinion flips, \(q_j \mapsto -q_j\), iff

\[-u < \bar\sigma_j < u \quad\text{and}\quad |\sigma_j| > l.\]

The first condition says the neighborhood is not yet committed; the second says this particular player has drifted far enough out to reconsider. \(T\) is the only dimensionless timescale in the problem, and the pair \((l, u)\) — with \(\Delta = u - l > 0\) — carve out the change-of-heart regions \([-u, -l] \cup [l, u]\) where flips can fire.

Running a game

A Game binds a graph to the three parameters:

import pycoop as pc

game = pc.Game(pc.erg_connected(20, 0.1, rng=0), u=0.5, l=0.2, T=0.1)
traj = game.run(rng=1, max_steps=200_000)

run accepts initial conditions (sigma0, q0; both default to uniform draws), a step budget, a time budget (max_time), and a seed. It halts early when the state is absorbed — every action pinned at its opinion’s wall with every agreement window shut, so no flip can ever fire again. Consensus is the absorbing corner where all the signs agree; the other absorbing corners are where fraught and obdurate states live.

The trajectory

Everything the run did comes back in a Trajectory — event times, the full action and opinion arrays after every event, who fired, and whether they flipped. The randomness of the whole model is the iid sequence \((\tau_k, j_k)\), so the trajectory is the run; every observable in the next guide is computed from it after the fact, and nothing is baked into the simulator.

The clock as a knob

The exponential clock is exact, but it is not sacred. Pass a float and every wait becomes a fixed step \(h\); pass a callable and the waits follow any law you like:

game = pc.Game(pc.complete(64), u=0.5, l=0.2, T=2.0, step_law=0.03125)

The fixed-step variant is a genuinely different model — positions live on a lattice of spacing \(h\), and step-size dispersion enters only through the resolution near the thresholds. Whether that matters is an empirical question, and it bears directly on whether the model’s critical behavior is a property of the dynamics or an artifact of the clock. If the phenomenology is invariant to the clock law, then the timescale is effectively a non-parameter — which is exactly the kind of thing worth checking rather than assuming.
