The Observables

A trajectory is just data until you ask it questions. This guide covers the questions pyCoop knows how to ask — what a state is, how content each player is, and how the answers add up over a run.

Agreement

For player \(i\), agreement is the absolute value of the local mean action,

\[a_i = |\bar\sigma_i| \in [0, 1],\]

computed by agreement(). Note what the absolute value hides: a crowd torn between extremes and a crowd of uniform fence-sitters get scored as equally agreeable, which feels wrong — two very different neighborhoods, one polarized and one lukewarm, graded alike. That lack is what the alignment asymmetry below is built to address.

Friends, enemies, and fraughtness

Fix a state with no player exactly at zero. Split player \(i\)’s neighbors by the sign of their action — opinions play no role here. The friends share \(i\)’s sign, the enemies oppose it, and each camp gets a weight,

\[F_i = \Big|\sum_{j \in \mathrm{friends}(i)} \sigma_j\Big|, \qquad E_i = \Big|\sum_{j \in \mathrm{enemies}(i)} \sigma_j\Big|,\]

whose difference \(y_i = F_i - E_i\) is the alignment asymmetry (alignment_asymmetry()). A positive \(y_i\) says player \(i\)’s friends outweigh their enemies — no local incentive to defect.

A state is fraught when three things hold at once: no player sits at zero, the crowd is split, and every \(y_i \geq 0\). The plain reading is a standoff — everyone is locally content, yet the group has failed to coordinate. This is exactly a non-Pareto-optimal Nash equilibrium (Tabor, Smith, and Dankowicz, 2024; Conrad and Tabor, 2024), and the states are tense but stable — fraught, not frustrated. Fraughtness needs at least four players: on the paw it occupies a sliver of volume \(1/384\) of the hypercube, on the four-path a healthier \(1/32\), and pyCoop’s is_fraught() reproduces both fractions by Monte Carlo.

Obdurateness

The other way to fail. A state is obdurate when the crowd is split, no player is at zero, and some player has \(y_i < 0\) — that player could improve their own agreement by switching sides, but the dynamics won’t let them (their agreement is already above threshold, so their window is shut). The blame for the jam falls on individual holdouts — each enjoying high agreement bought by their neighbors’ coordination while contributing nothing to it. is_obdurate() tests the condition, and classify() sorts any state into consensus, fraught, obdurate, or indeterminate.

Over a whole run

classify_trajectory() labels every event state, and state_fractions() weights the labels by the time spent in them — the fraction of a run spent in each condition:

import pycoop as pc
from pycoop import observables as obs

game = pc.Game(pc.erg_connected(10, 0.1, rng=0), u=0.3, l=0.5, T=0.1)
traj = game.run(rng=1)
print(obs.state_fractions(traj))

On the complete graph there is one more number worth extracting. While the window is open, flips only ever depolarize the crowd, and the mean opinion decays at a rate set by the fraction of players past the position threshold,

\[\alpha = 1 - \frac{2l}{T + 2},\]

a geometric occupancy fraction — which is why it holds still. alpha_estimate() measures it from a trajectory; on \(K_{64}\) the estimate matches the formula to four digits. Voila!
