## Charts and conventions

We use mostly plus signature and take the null coordinate $u$ to have dimensions of time, $v$ and every transverse coordinate to have dimensions of length, and $\mu>0$ to have dimensions of inverse length.
Components use $x^0=cu$; a component labelled $u$ therefore refers to $cu$.
Put $\omega_i=\mu/3$ for $i=1,2,3$ and $\omega_i=\mu/6$ for $i=4,\ldots,9$.
Figueroa-O'Farrill and Papadopoulos's $x^-=cu$ and $x^+=-v$ fix the sign of the mixed term.

### Brinkmann coordinates

The global chart is $(u,v,x_1,\ldots,x_9)$ with every coordinate real.
With $H=\sum_i\omega_i^2x_i^2$, the line element is
$$ds^2=-2c\,du\,dv-Hc^2du^2+\sum_{i=1}^9dx_i^2.$$
The 4-form in the same convention is
$$F=\mu c\,du\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3.$$
Thus $F_{u123}=\mu$ in the component basis with first coordinate $cu$.

### Rosen coordinates

Use $(u,w,y_1,\ldots,y_9)$ with $0<u<3\pi/(\mu c)$ and the remaining coordinates real.
Let $a_i=\sin(\omega_i cu)$, with $a_i'$ denoting differentiation with respect to $cu$.
The map is
$$x_i=a_i y_i,\qquad v=w+\frac12\sum_i a_i a_i'y_i^2.$$
The line element is
$$ds^2=-2c\,du\,dw+\sum_i a_i^2dy_i^2.$$
The interval ends when the first three scale factors vanish; the Brinkmann geometry continues smoothly.
The 4-form is $F=\mu c\,a_1a_2a_3\,du\wedge dy_1\wedge dy_2\wedge dy_3$.
The factor of two, sign of the null coordinate, and transverse constant rescalings relative to hep-th/0201081 (14)-(17) follow from the displayed map.
No conformally flat chart of the isotropic IIB wave applies to this anisotropic geometry.
