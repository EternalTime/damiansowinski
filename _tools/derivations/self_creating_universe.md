# Gott and Li's self-creating universe

The four charts of `self_creating_universe.json` are written by `print_charts.py --metric self_creating_universe`, which computes every tensor from each line element with the checker's own `Geometry` and reads every printed value back before it writes.
This note records the source of each chart, the identification in each, and what the diagrams draw.
Every equation number is of `gott1998`, J. Richard Gott, III and Li-Xin Li, "Can the Universe create itself?", Phys. Rev. D 58, 023501 (1998), section IX, read in the arXiv copy astro-ph/9712344.

## Step 1. The spacetime

De Sitter space is the hyperboloid $W^2 + X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2 - V^2 = r_0^2$ in flat space of five dimensions, (61) and (62), with $r_0 = \sqrt{3/\Lambda}$.
Gott and Li identify each event with its images under the boost $V \to V\cosh nb + W\sinh nb$, $W \to W\cosh nb + V\sinh nb$ for every integer $n$, and write the quotient dS/B.
Their $\beta = br_0$, (83), is a length, and the published charts take it for the parameter, with $c$ restored.
Every local quantity is de Sitter's: $R_{\mu\nu} = (3/r_0^2)g_{\mu\nu}$, no Weyl tensor, $R = 12/r_0^2$ and $K = 24/r_0^4$.

The boost fixes the sphere $V = W = 0$, $X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2 = r_0^2$, where the quotient is no manifold, and the null surfaces $W = \pm V$ through it are the Cauchy horizons, generated by closed null geodesics.
They cut the hyperboloid into four regions, (65) to (68): R, $W > |V|$, and L, $W < -|V|$, where the orbits of the boost are timelike and every event lies on a closed timelike curve, and F, $V > |W|$, and P, $V < -|W|$, with no closed causal curve.

## Step 2. The charts

With $n$ the unit vector of $\theta$ and $\phi$:

- `static`, (63) and (64): $V = \sqrt{r_0^2 - r^2}\sinh(ct/r_0)$, $W = \sqrt{r_0^2 - r^2}\cosh(ct/r_0)$, $(X, Y, Z) = r\,n$. It covers R, and the boost is $ct \to ct + \beta$, so $t$ has the period $\beta/c$ and every line of constant $r$, $\theta$ and $\phi$ is a closed timelike curve of proper length $\beta\sqrt{1 - r^2/r_0^2}$.
- `kantowski_sachs`, (72) and (73): $V = r_0\sinh(c\tau/r_0)\cosh(l/r_0)$, $W = r_0\sinh(c\tau/r_0)\sinh(l/r_0)$, $(X, Y, Z) = r_0\cosh(c\tau/r_0)\,n$. It covers F, and the boost is $l \to l + \beta$. Gott and Li call it "a type of Kantowski-Sachs universe". Their (70) is the static line element continued to $r > r_0$ by (69), $r \to \tilde t$ and $t \to l$, and (71), $\tilde t = r_0\cosh(\tau/r_0)$, carries it to (72); it is not published as a chart of its own, and the static plane is drawn on through the horizon, where it is (70).
- `steady_state`, (74) and (75): $W + V = r_0e^{c\tau/r_0}$ and $(X, Y, Z) = e^{c\tau/r_0}(x, y, z)$. It covers R, F and the horizon between them, the half $W + V > 0$, and the boost multiplies $W + V$ by $e^{b}$ and leaves $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ alone, so it is $c\tau \to c\tau + \beta$ with $x$, $y$ and $z$ shrunk by $e^{-\beta/r_0}$. One period of $\tau$ is a fundamental domain. The Cauchy horizon $W = V$ is $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = r_0^2e^{-2c\tau/r_0}$.
- `conformal`, (76) and (77): $\eta = -r_0e^{-c\tau/r_0}$ and $\rho^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$, on which the boost is the dilation of $\eta$ and $\rho$ by $e^{-\beta/r_0}$ and the horizon is $\rho = -\eta$.

Gott and Li use $\tau$ both for the proper time of (72) and for the steady state time of (75), and the two charts keep that letter.
`self_creating_universe_check` holds each chart to the hyperboloid's metric pulled back along these, to $R_{\mu\nu} = (3/r_0^2)g_{\mu\nu}$, to a vanishing Weyl tensor, to its stated identification being the boost, and to the region named here; `SCU_BOOST` in `print_charts.py` holds the four maps.

## Step 3. The numbers the page quotes

The region R of one copy has the four dimensional volume $\tfrac{4}{3}\pi br_0^4 = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi\beta r_0^3$, since $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$ in the static chart.
The stress energy of a conformally coupled scalar field in the adapted conformal Rindler vacuum is (95), finite on the Cauchy horizon if and only if $\beta = 2\pi r_0$, (96), when it is $-g_{ab}/(960\pi^2r_0^4)$, (97).
A light ray of the Kantowski-Sachs plane keeps $l \pm r_0\ln\tanh(c\tau/2r_0)$, so one sent at $\tau$ covers the stretch $-r_0\ln\tanh(c\tau/2r_0)$ of $l$ in all the time after it, and that is less than $\beta/2$ once $\tau > \tau_0 = (r_0/c)\ln\frac{e^{b/2} + 1}{e^{b/2} - 1}$, which is $0.086\,r_0/c$ at $b = 2\pi$ (their section IX F).

## Step 4. The drawings

Every spacetime diagram and the embedding diagram are drawn at $r_0 = 1$ and $\beta = 2\pi r_0$.
The static chart is drawn along a line through the observer over one period of $t$; the Kantowski-Sachs plane over one period of $l$; the steady state plane over one period of $\tau$, with the Cauchy horizon $x = \pm r_0e^{-c\tau/r_0}$ marked; and the conformal plane on the triangle below future infinity, with the horizon $\rho = -\eta$ marked.
`null_rays.py --verify` checks the rays against $ct \pm r_0\,\mathrm{artanh}(r/r_0)$, $l \pm r_0\ln\tanh(c\tau/2r_0)$, $x \mp r_0e^{-c\tau/r_0}$ and $\eta \pm \rho$.

The conformal diagram is de Sitter's square, $V = r_0\tan T$, $W = r_0\cos\chi/\cos T$ and $\sqrt{X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2} = r_0\sin\chi/\cos T$, with one view per chart, each tinting one copy between two lines the boost carries onto one another.
At $\beta = 2\pi r_0$ one copy fills the square to within a pixel, so those views are drawn at $\beta = r_0$, as Misner's are drawn at a boost of rapidity 1.
`conformal.py --verify` holds each chart to the published metric, to the hyperboloid, and to its identification being the boost.

The embedding diagram is the slice $\phi = 0$ of a moment of constant $\tau$ in F, whose metric $r_0^2\cosh^2(c\tau/r_0)\,d\theta^2 + \sinh^2(c\tau/r_0)\,dl^2$ is that of a flat cylinder of circumference $\beta\sinh(c\tau/r_0)$ and length $\pi r_0\cosh(c\tau/r_0)$, drawn at $c\tau = 0.25$, $0.5$, $1$ and $1.5$ times $r_0$ and played as a movie.
On the steady state plane the moment $\tau_k$ is $x = r_0\cosh(c\tau_k/r_0)e^{-c\tau/r_0}$, and on the conformal plane the line $\rho = -\eta\cosh(c\tau_k/r_0)$; `slices.py` checks both maps by pulling one published metric back onto the other.
No moment of F meets the static patch.
`_tools/test_self_creating_universe.py` holds the published files to all of this.
