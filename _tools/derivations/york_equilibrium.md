# A black hole in thermal equilibrium with scalar radiation

## The scalar field and the metric

The published APS record identifies York's 1985 first order back reaction and credits Page's approximation, while its full text remains inaccessible.
Hochberg, Kephart and York's author preprint, arXiv:gr-qc/9307037, Eqs. (6)-(12) and (17)-(19), supplies the explicit metric functions and the matched reflecting wall.
In units $G=c=1$, the horizon is at $2M$, the mass function is $m(r)=M[1+\epsilon\mu(r)]$, $\epsilon=\hbar/M^2$, and $K=3840\pi$.
The redshift factor uses $\rho(r)-\rho(R)$, with $R$ the reflecting wall's radius.
All metric tensors and scalars are retained only through first order in $\epsilon$, even where the line element writes a reciprocal or product.
The boundary matches to a Schwarzschild exterior of mass $m(R)$; the jump in extrinsic curvature represents the wall, which is not included in the smooth interior chart.

Taylor and Breen, Physical Review D 103, 025006 (2021), Eq. (42), supplies Page's scalar stress in a form whose zero cosmological constant limit independently reproduces the derivatives of $\mu$ and $\rho$.
The field stress is the infinite space approximation; York's 1993 author preprint, arXiv:gr-qc/9307022, p. 6, explains the need for a sufficiently large wall and estimates a characteristic scalar wavelength near $42M$.
The difference between Hochberg's preprint prose and its energy formulas is recorded in the task literature ledger, and neither disputed radius is asserted here.

## Checks and drawings

The chart printer holds $\mu$ and $\rho$ as functions while it forms the tensors, and truncates every result through $\epsilon^1$.
Its point checks compare the mass and redshift derivatives with Page's stress as written by Taylor and Breen, and check the horizon integration constants and the matching of $\rho$ at the wall.
The dedicated test reads the printed components, checks the zero-field Schwarzschild limit and the wall lapse, and independently checks Page's energy-density relation at four radii.

The drawings take $M=1$, $R=50M$ and $\epsilon=10^{-4}$.
The cavity extends from $2M$ to its reflecting wall, farther out than York's characteristic wavelength, and the correction remains small there.
The equatorial surface uses the radial component of the published first order metric and stops at the wall.
The conformal view integrates $dr_*/dr=\sqrt{g_{rr}/(-g_{tt})}$ from the wall and compactifies the cavity alone, with no continuation assumed inside the horizon.
The curvature plots read the printed scalar formulas and keep the same expansion order.
