# Stringy cosmic strings

## Sources

Harvey and Royston, arXiv:0709.1482, section 2.2, equations (2.3)-(2.7), write the modular factor and its transverse metric in a ten dimensional seven brane geometry.
Holding the six additional flat directions fixed leaves the four dimensional sector.
Their modular invariant is normalized by $j(i)=1$.
Gibbons, Green, and Perry, arXiv:hep-th/9511080, section 3, write polar coordinates for the transverse space and give the single pole asymptotic deficit $\pi/6$.

## Coordinate systems

The Cartesian transverse chart uses $z=x+iy$.
The polar transverse chart uses $z=re^{i\phi}$.
The source's modular factor depends on both transverse coordinates in a general multi string configuration.

## Geometry and drawing decisions

At a simple pole the eta factor cancels the explicit power of distance, leaving logarithmic behavior at the core.
At large distance in the finite limiting modulus family, the transverse factor falls as $r^{-N/6}$.
The total asymptotic or integrated deficit is $N\pi/6$; the twelve pole case is asymptotically cylindrical and the regular twenty four pole case closes on a sphere.
Further chart, drawing and absent diagram decisions remain to be recorded as they are implemented.

The diagrams of the transverse cone use the constant modulus limit of the six string configuration in Bergshoeff, Hartong, Ortin and Roest, arXiv:hep-th/0612072, section 6.4.
In that limit the six pole positions coincide, the remaining transverse deficit is $\pi$, and the local factor may be normalized to $H=\ell/r$ away from the common point.
This is a limiting cone, not the logarithmic core of a separated simple pole; its diagrams stop at a positive radius.
The two charts leave $H$ as a function so the printed tensors apply to the entire sourced family.
Their independent point checks substitute the local solution $\tau=z$ and $H=\operatorname{Im}z$ and verify its Ricci field equation in both coordinate systems.

The curvature figure instead uses the local solution $\tau=z/\ell$ and $H=y/\ell$ on $y>0$, which solves the stated field equation.
It is labelled as a local solution and is not the six string cone shown in the other figures.
The chart expressions retain general derivatives of $H$, and the curvature program substitutes the local choice into those stored expressions.
