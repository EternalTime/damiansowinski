# Counter rotating discs

## Sources and construction

Jiří Bičák, Donald Lynden-Bell and Joseph Katz, Physical Review D 47, 4334-4343 (1993), establish the static Weyl disc sources.
The founding primary abstract and bibliographic record were fetched; full text remains an explicit access debt after APS PDF and harvest returned 401 and ADS returned 403.
The primary abstract names Curzon, Schwarzschild and Zipoy-Voorhees among the examples.
Bičák and Tomáš Ledvinka, Physical Review Letters 71, 1669-1672 (1993), equations (1)-(6), give the stationary metric, reflection and junction stress; their equations (12)-(14) give the equal-stream speed and proper densities.
All four pages of the author-hosted scanned PDF were read visually.
Ledvinka and Bičák, Physical Review D 99, 064046 (2019), equations (2)-(16), (26)-(33), and Appendix A, give the junction, energy conditions, geodesic streams and static field equations.
The fetched arXiv version is 1903.01726v1.
The 2019 discussion on page 6 explicitly distinguishes the 1993 equal-density decomposition from geodesic streams and states that the former needs radial momentum exchange.
José P. S. Lemos and Patricio S. Letelier, Physical Review D 49, 5135-5143 (1994), are cited only for the primary abstract's black-hole superposition and its last annular family.
Their full text is a declared access debt, not evidence for any equation here.
Their black hole and annular disc are a superposition, with an inner edge and central black hole retained.
This is distinct from excising a Kerr horizon inside a displaced slab.

The seed coordinate is Z=b+|z|, with b>0 chosen above the singular region.
The upper half uses Z=b+z and the lower half Z=b-z.
The first fundamental form agrees across z=0.
The two cooriented normal derivatives have opposite signs.
The spacetime includes both retained regions and their material junction; it is not the seed vacuum manifold extended across the cut.

## Charts and smooth tensors

The static Weyl chart has ds²=-exp(2U)dt²+exp(2k-2U)(dρ²+dz²)+ρ²exp(-2U)dφ².
The source is the 2019 equation (1) with A=0 and the static field equations (57)-(59); the 2000 author review, section 6.1, independently states the same form.
The stationary canonical chart has ds²=-f(dt+A dφ)²+(g/f)(dρ²+dz²)+(ρ²/f)dφ², the 1993 equation (1).
All of their potentials are composed with Z.
The smooth tensor expressions retain the potentials as free functions, as the canonical Weyl chart of the Morgan-Morgan discs does.
The expressions apply for z≠0; their Ricci expressions vanish for the vacuum seed equations.
They do not encode a Dirac measure as a smooth function or square it in a curvature invariant.
Every tensor is generated by print_charts.py and read back by verify_metrics.py.
The chart check compares the metric at points with the source line element and performs no symbolic determinant or simplify.
The global static tag is overruled because the stationary Kerr members have angular momentum; the static chart is a subfamily.
The global spacetime is not vacuum because of its seam.

For Curzon, U=-m/sqrt(ρ²+Z²) and k=-m²ρ²/[2(ρ²+Z²)²].
For a Weyl rod of half length a, R±=sqrt(ρ²+(Z±a)²), W=R++R-, U=(δ/2)log((W-2a)/(W+2a)), and k=(δ²/2)log((W²-4a²)/(4R+R-)).
Schwarzschild is δ=1 and a=m; general δ is Zipoy-Voorhees with mass δa.
These are seed formulas; composition with Z creates the disc sources.
The 1993 static abstract and the 2000 review section 6.1 establish the seed identifications.

For Kerr, set σ=m²-a² and u=[ρ²+Z²+σ+sqrt((ρ²+Z²+σ)²-4σZ²)]/2.
Then r=m+sqrt(u), y=Z/sqrt(u), Σ=r²+a²y² and D=Σ-2mr.
The canonical functions are f=D/Σ, g=D/(u-σy²) and A=2mar(1-y²)/D.
These follow by eliminating x,y from the 1993 equations (2)-(3) and work for positive, zero and negative σ on the retained exterior branch.
The dedicated checks include both a<m and a>m.
The central density is m[(b+m)²-a²]/[2π sqrt(b²+a²-m²)((b+m)²+a²)^(3/2)], from page 1671 of the 1993 Kerr paper.

## Junction and matter

Use G=c=1 and one normal pointing from the lower region to the upper region.
On the seam h_ab is the induced metric and [K_ab]=(∂z g_ab at 0+)/sqrt(g_zz), with a,b=t,ρ,φ.
The surface stress is S_ab=-([K_ab]-h_ab [K])/(8π), the 2019 equation (10) with its jump orientation reversed accordingly.
For the static metric, k_z=2ρU_ρU_z implies S_ρρ=0.
The orthonormal energy and azimuthal pressure are ε=exp(U-k)U_z(1-ρU_ρ)/(2π) and pφ=exp(U-k)U_z ρU_ρ/(2π).
These are the 2019 equations (11)-(14) specialized to planar cuts and the static field equation (58).
They are not claimed as a transcription of inaccessible equations in the 1993 static paper.

Two streams with normalized velocities γ(e_t±v e_φ) and equal proper rest surface densities s have S=2sγ²(e_t e_t+v² e_φ e_φ).
Thus v²=pφ/ε and s=(ε-pφ)/2, the 1993 equations (12)-(14).
The physical dust case ε>pφ≥0 has positive proper density, timelike velocities and satisfies the weak and dominant energy conditions.
For the reflected Curzon example b=3m, ρU_ρ has maximum 2m/(3 sqrt(3)b), below 1/2 everywhere.
This bound also holds with b=m, used by the density checks.
In a static seed, the circular geodesic equation independently reproduces the same speed.
For Kerr, the geodesic roots solve gφφ,ρ Ω²+2gtφ,ρ Ω+gtt,ρ=0, the 2019 equation (32).
Their normalizations and positive densities must be checked separately; the dedicated checks reconstruct all components of S from the two unequal streams.
They do not infer geodesic motion from the equal-stream speed alone.

## Embedding drawings

The matter plane at t=z=0, with Curzon seed and b=3m, has intrinsic line element exp(2k-2U)dρ²+ρ²exp(-2U)dφ².
Its Euclidean radius is ρ exp(-U) and its height slope is the square root of exp(2k-2U)-(d(ρ exp(-U))/dρ)².
The surface begins at its regular axis and ends at the displayed ρ=6m; the physical disc extends onward.
The second surface is the cylinder ρ=2m at t=0, with coordinates z and φ.
Its Euclidean radius is 2m exp(-U) and its height slope is the square root of exp(2k-2U)-(d(2m exp(-U))/dz)².
The lower half is the reflection of the upper half, and both meet on their matter circle at z=0.
The displayed cylinder reaches -2m≤z≤2m.
Both profiles use the stored metric and pass the shared chord, meridian and circumference isometry checks.
No new CSS class is introduced.

## Light and moments

The static radial view is the reflection plane z=0, with rays of the induced metric and the symmetric normal prescription at the thin seam.
The static axial view crosses the material junction and has dt/dz=±exp(2m/(b+|z|)).
The stationary axial view uses the Kerr restriction f=(Z²+a²-m²)/((Z+m)²+a²), g=1, A=0 at a=0.8m and b=3m.
These axis values and their transverse first derivatives agree with the full Kerr expressions: all transverse first derivatives vanish there.
Only the axial restriction is needed for these null geodesics; no off-axis curvature is inferred from that restricted extension.
All ray families are checked against independent quadratures of their null slopes.
The matter plane's t=0 moment is registered on the static radial view from ρ=0 through 6m, and as the centre point on the static axis.
The cylinder's moment meets the radial plane on its ρ=2m circle, which projects to one marked point.
The cylinder does not meet the symmetry axis; its absence there is declared in the scoped hidden_views exception.
The stationary axial drawing uses a Kerr seed and neither Curzon embedding lies in that member of the family; the scoped hidden exception records this distinction.
Each registration carries the embedding view ID and surface stamp.
Each embedding contains one moment and needs no movie.
