  <h1>A.I. Literacy for S.T.E.M. Education</h1>
  <p class="aistem-subtitle">A resource in our rapidly changing academic world on using AI agents efficiently, critically, and ethically while studying and doing research. Our summer workshop is a cross-disciplinary initiative for undergraduates to learn how to apply their S.T.E.M. thinking to create rewarding collaborations with our new, in-silico friends! </p>
  <a class="aistem-btn" href="/AISTEM/signup/">Sign Up</a>
</div>

<div class="aistem-section">
  <h2>About the Workshop</h2>
  <p>Agentic AI is an inevitable fact of our future. Learning how to use these remarkable tools efficiently and ethically is the goal of this program. Working hand in hand with agents, you will dive into the academic literature to produce a historically comprehensive review on a STEM topic of your own choosing.</p>
  <p>You will learn to identify when models hallucinate and confabulate, develop workflows that catch such occurrences, and tune agents to become better research assistants. By working on a subject you find deeply interesting,  you will gain the context to jump into more quantitative research in the near future.</p>
</div>

<hr class="aistem-divider">

<div class="aistem-section">
  <h2>What You Will Do</h2>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Set Up a Research Project with an LLM</h3>
    <p>Learn the organizational architecture that entails — how to scope a project, set goalposts, and maintain a productive working relationship with an agent over weeks of sustained research.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Maintain an Active Bibliography</h3>
    <p>Use Zotero and Overleaf to keep a living bibliography of your topic. You will learn to skim, read in depth, and connect ideas across the literature — with mindmaps tracking how the papers relate to one another.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Keep a Journal of Interactions</h3>
    <p>Document your exchanges with LLMs — noting issues, confabulations, and how you dealt with them. Journals are presented and discussed weekly. Write these down. Keep a small journal full of questions, and then talk about them.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Present Your Findings</h3>
    <p>Deliver a 15-minute presentation on your chosen topic — the historical and technical narrative you uncovered — drawing on your bibliography and journal as primary sources.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="aistem-section">
  <h2>Example Topics</h2>
  <p>Students choose their own topic. The goal is to work on something deeply interesting to you — that context is what makes the research real. Some examples to get you thinking:</p>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Neutrinos</h3>
    <p>When were these ephemeral particles first proposed and when were they discovered? What experiments have probed them in the past 70 years? What details have we learned about them, and what does that mean for the evolution of our Universe?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>The Living Microcosm</h3>
    <p>The physical regimes tiny organisms inhabit are vastly different than our own. What is the world like for these single-celled creatures? What skills have they evolved — chemoreception, information transfer in noisy environments, cell locomotion — to make tehm the most numerous lifeforms on our planet?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Wormholes and Time Machines</h3>
    <p>When were the first solutions containing closed timelike curves found in Einstein's theory of General Relativity? Examine the historical developement of dust solutions, the Gödel universe, and exotic objects like cosmic strings. Are these solutions describing things that actually exist, or are their physical constraints needed from outside the General Theory?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Seeing Back to the Beginning</h3>
    <p>How old is the Universe? How big is the Universe? How has science illuminated these questions and shed light on our place in the Cosmos? From Cepheids to the Hubble flow, what have we learned, and waht questions remain?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="aistem-card">
    <h3>Life as a Complex System</h3>
    <p>How has physics influenced biology? From the discovery of power laws in arterial branching to harnessing thermal fluctuations by cellular machines, physical ideas have revealed deep structural principles that underlie living systems. What are some of these principles, and what is teh story of how they were discovered?</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="aistem-section">
  <h2>Format</h2>
  <p>The planned workshop will run for several weeks over the summer — timing is negotiable. The first week will be learning the ropes, installing software, and becoming comfortable with your digital workspace. We will set up Overleaf and Zotero accounts, discuss and pick research topics, and learn how to set up a project using an LLM. Each student will meet one on one with an instructor to get them started.</p>
  <p>The following weeks will consist of on-site active learning — reading, analyzing, and discussing papers in the scientific literature, fine tuning LLM models through highly-organized project structures, mapping out the historical trajectory of your chosen scientific topic, and developping the skills to assess your LLMs. The workshop will end with presentations by each participant in the form of public lectures. Both in-person and remote participation via Zoom are available.</p>
