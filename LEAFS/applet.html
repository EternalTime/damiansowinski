Applet

The 2d world of pyLEAFS, running live in your browser. It is the world forager() builds, with the same rules and parameters, applied in the same order: the births and deaths of ResourceField, region by region, then the steering, motion, harvest, metabolism, budding and death of Population, stage by stage. Nothing is precomputed.

The applet is yours to take apart. It lives in the library’s repository as docs/_static/leafs_applet.js: plain JavaScript with no build step and no dependencies. The first part is the model, ported line for line from the modules it documents, along with the samplers behind its random numbers. The rest draws the world and its two panels. All of the model’s randomness passes through one function, draw, so you can feed it the draws of a seeded Python run and compare the two step by step.

Green dots are resources and pink disks are foragers, each sized by its fuel, with a short line showing its heading. The world starts where the Python one does: the field near its equilibrium of \(N_\mathrm{eq} = \Gamma L^2/(\epsilon\gamma)\) resources per region, and five foragers with full tanks, which bud on the first step. The top panel on the right follows the census, each population divided by its carrying capacity, \(N_A^{\max} = \Gamma V/\mu_0\) for the foragers and \(N_R^{\max} = \Gamma\tau_E V/\epsilon\) for the resources: the foragers boom, graze the field down, and settle. Below it the phase plane shows the same run on log axes, with the equilibrium line \(N_R/N_R^{\max} + N_A/N_A^{\max} = 1\) of The model dashed.

The census settles on that line wherever the foragers survive, as long as \(\Xi\) stays above about 0.2. At steady state the energy flowing in, \(\Gamma V\), is either burned by the foragers, \(\mu_0 N_A\), or lost when a resource decays, \(\gamma\epsilon N_R\), and the sliders move the point along the line. Toward the bottom of the \(\Xi\) slider a single resource is worth most of a tank, and the energy that spills past a full one is lost too, so the census sits under the line. Narrow the sensing radius toward \(r_\mathrm{col}\) and the foragers, nearly blind, find food by bumping into it; they still get by, but fewer of them, on a fuller field. Speed them up and they graze the field bare and close in on \(N_A^{\max}\). The homogeneity \(\Xi\) decides how the same energy is divided: slide it down for a few rich resources far apart, up for a pasture of lean ones, and watch the census swing harder in the sparse world.

Tap a forager to follow it. A ring marks it, a faint circle its sensing radius, and a pink line its recent path, and the readout adds its fuel, age, harvest and offspring. Tap empty ground to drop in a new forager there, with a full tank and a random heading, as a click does in the Viewer. In Python the parameters are fixed when the world is built; here the sliders act on the running world, and Reset builds a fresh one from their values.
